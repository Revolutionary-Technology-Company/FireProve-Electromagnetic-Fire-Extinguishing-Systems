Transitioning the entire structural and acoustic profile to Grade 5 Titanium (Ti-6Al-4V) provides the ultimate high-melting-point defense, surviving structural heat up to 1,660°C (3,020°F). Building a custom acoustic compression driver entirely out of titanium ensures the turret can operate inside intense thermal radiation zones.
## 1. Ultra-Thin Titanium Diaphragm & Compliance
Standard speaker surrounds (rubber, foam, or silk) will instantly fail near open flames. To survive, the speaker's compliance (the moving suspension ring) and the central dome must be forged from a single, contiguous sheet of foil-gauge titanium.

* 
* Material Spec: Use 0.02 mm to 0.05 mm ultra-thin titanium foil. Titanium has an exceptional strength-to-weight ratio and high elasticity when stamped into thin gauges.
* Corrugated Compliance Ring: Stamping concentric, micrometer-deep ripples into the outer perimeter of the titanium sheet creates a flexible, spring-like compliance ring without using polymer joints. This allows the stiff center dome to plunge linearly and push low-frequency sound waves while maintaining airtight thermal resilience.
* The Voice Coil Joint: The moving voice coil wire must use high-temperature copper-clad aluminum wire (CCAW) wrapped around a Kapton or ceramic former, bonded to the titanium dome using specialized high-temp aerospace vitreous enamel or ceramic adhesive rated past 1000°C.
*

* Compression Chamber: The thin titanium diaphragm sits micron-distances away from a solid titanium phasing plug. The diaphragm compresses the air in this tight gap, forcing it through narrow acoustic channels into the 4.8-inch barrel throat.
* Pneumatic Amplification: This compression acts like a physical transformer, multiplying the acoustic pressure exit velocity, allowing the inverse sound waves ($W_i$) to punch through turbulent fire convective currents.

## 3. Thermal Shielding & Magnetic Safety
While titanium won't melt in typical fire thresholds, the driver's permanent magnets will fail if they reach their Curie Temperature (the point where magnets permanently lose their fields).

* 
* Magnetic Shielding: Neodymium magnets degrade around 320°C. Therefore, the magnet assembly must be completely encased inside a thick, sealed titanium cooling jacket filled with a liquid metal thermal interface or liquid coolant.
* Barrel Dissipation: The 4.8-inch titanium rifled barrel acts as a colossal heatsink. Because titanium has lower thermal conductivity than steel, it slows the inward crawl of heat toward the driver base, keeping the voice coil gap stable during continuous frequency generation.
